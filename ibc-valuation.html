Section 4.4

Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)

CIRP is a time-bound legal process for resolving the insolvency of a corporate debtor through a resolution plan, restructuring or other lawful outcome. Where resolution is unsuccessful, liquidation may follow.

Professional Support May Include:

  • Preliminary case and document review
  • Compilation of supporting records
  • Application and reply documentation support
  • Preparation and verification of creditor claims
  • Assistance to corporate debtors, creditors and stakeholders
  • Corporate and ROC compliance support after resolution

Brief CIRP Steps Overview:

1
Identification of Debt & Default

Verification of financial or operational debt default and legal eligibility.

2
Issue of Demand Notice

Issuance of statutory Form 3/Form 4 demand notice by operational creditor, where applicable.

3
Application Filing before NCLT

Preparation and filing of CIRP application under Section 7, 9 or 10 before NCLT bench.

4
Admission & IRP Appointment

Order of admission by NCLT, initiation of moratorium, and appointment of Interim Resolution Professional (IRP).

5
Public Announcement & Claims

Public announcement inviting claims from financial, operational, employee, and statutory creditors.

6
Constitution of CoC

Verification of claims and formal constitution of the Committee of Creditors (CoC).

7
Invitation of Resolution Plans

Issuance of Expression of Interest (EOI) and evaluation of resolution plans submitted by prospective resolution applicants.

8
Approval of Resolution Plan

Voting and approval of resolution plan by CoC, followed by sanction from Adjudicating Authority (NCLT).

9
Liquidation (Where Applicable)

Initiation of liquidation process if resolution plan is not approved or is unsuccessful.

Section 5

Valuation — Information Only

Valuation helps determine the reasonable value of a business, shares, securities or assets for legal, financial and commercial decision-making. It may be relevant in insolvency, liquidation, restructuring, fundraising, issue or transfer of shares, mergers, acquisitions, joint ventures, business expansion and investor induction.

Situations Where Valuation May Be Required:

  • Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process
  • Liquidation and voluntary liquidation
  • Sale or realisation of assets
  • Evaluation of resolution plans
  • Business restructuring
  • Merger or acquisition
  • Issue or transfer of shares
  • Preferential allotment and private placement
  • Start-up fundraising and investor induction
  • Business expansion and joint ventures
  • Promoter or shareholder restructuring
